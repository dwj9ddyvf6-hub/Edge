## Information and game theory

### I01 — Grossman and Stiglitz (1980), On the Impossibility of Informationally Efficient Markets

Sanford J. Grossman; Joseph E. Stiglitz. *American Economic Review* 70(3), 393–408. [Institutional record](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/D8765R99).

**Access:** institutional record and available primary-paper material inspected. **Supports:** costly information creates a tension between fully revealing prices and incentives to acquire information. Residual informativeness can compensate information production. **Limit:** this equilibrium mechanism does not establish any particular signal's excess return; gross compensation may only repay acquisition and implementation costs. Require an explicitly costed information advantage.

### I02 — Kyle (1985), Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading

Albert S. Kyle. *Econometrica* 53(6), 1315–1335. [Publisher](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/browse/1985/11/01/continuous-auctions-and-insider-trading).

**Access:** indexed publisher abstract; PDF retrieval failed. **Supports:** informed strategic trading, noise demand, and competitive pricing jointly determine information revelation and price impact. **Limit:** the stylized market is not a modern execution simulator, and its theoretical informed trader is not permission to use unlawful information. A signal's value depends on attainable size, timing, and endogenous impact.

### I03 — Glosten and Milgrom (1985), Bid, Ask and Transaction Prices in a Specialist Market with Heterogeneously Informed Traders

Lawrence R. Glosten; Paul R. Milgrom. *Journal of Financial Economics* 14(1), 71–100. [Author institution](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/bid-ask-and-transaction-prices-specialist-market-heterogeneously-informed-traders); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(85)90044-3).

**Access:** primary institutional research record inspected. **Supports:** adverse selection can produce a positive spread despite a risk-neutral, zero-expected-profit specialist. **Limit:** quoted spread is not realizable profit; a fill changes the relevant conditioning information. Estimate fill-conditioned value and post-fill markouts. Do not deduct adverse selection twice if it already enters the payoff distribution.

### I04 — Milgrom and Stokey (1982), Information, Trade and Common Knowledge

Paul Milgrom; Nancy Stokey. *Journal of Economic Theory* 26(1), 17–27. [Author PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~milgrom/publishedarticles/Information%20Trade%20and%20Common%20Knowledge.pdf).

**Access:** author PDF and indexed publication record inspected. **Supports:** specified Pareto-efficiency, belief, and common-knowledge conditions prevent mutually acceptable speculative trade generated solely by private information. **Limit:** liquidity needs, constraints, incomplete markets, and different priors violate relevant assumptions. The theorem does not forbid real-world trading or require irrationality for profit; identify the proposed mechanism's broken assumption.

### I05 — Aumann (1976), Agreeing to Disagree

Robert J. Aumann. *Annals of Statistics* 4(6), 1236–1239. [Author institution](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/agreeing-to-disagree/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176343654).

**Access:** author institutional record and indexed original-paper material inspected. **Supports:** common-prior Bayesian agents cannot retain different event posteriors when those posteriors are common knowledge. **Limit:** market prices are not transparent beliefs, and theorem assumptions often fail. Diagnose disagreements through evidence, priors, likelihoods, payoff definitions, and constraints; repeated LLM agreement using the same evidence is not independent confirmation.

### I06 — Blackwell (1953), Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments

David Blackwell. *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 24(2), 265–272. [Publisher](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-24/issue-2/Equivalent-Comparisons-of-Experiments/10.1214/aoms/1177729032.full).

**Access:** publisher/indexed record inspected; publisher rendering limited full-text access. **Supports:** under its conditions, comparison of experiments by garbling corresponds to attainable decision payoff across decision problems. **Limit:** this is a partial ordering, not a claim that every extra datum helps a particular trade. Information costs and bounded-model representation quality must be analyzed separately from new external evidence.

### I07 — Sims (2003), Implications of Rational Inattention

Christopher A. Sims. *Journal of Monetary Economics* 50(3), 665–690. [Author institution](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/implications-of-rational-inattention/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3932(03)00029-1).

**Access:** institutional primary publication record inspected. **Supports:** finite information-processing capacity between observations and actions can generate behavior resembling adjustment frictions. **Limit:** a general attention model does not prove an exploitable anomaly. Specify the relevant information bottleneck, economic stakes, and action change; neglect can be rational when the attainable dollar opportunity is smaller than research and execution costs.

### I08 — Milgrom and Weber (1982), A Theory of Auctions and Competitive Bidding

Paul R. Milgrom; Robert J. Weber. *Econometrica* 50(5), 1089–1122. [University-hosted PDF](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr10/cos444/papers/milgrom_weber82.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1911865).

**Access:** primary-paper PDF and publication record inspected. **Supports:** affiliated signals and interdependent values make rival behavior informative; conditional valuation addresses winner's-curse effects. **Limit:** not every market order is a common-value auction. Ask what obtaining a fill, winning an allocation, or selecting the highest apparent opportunity reveals, and test performance conditional on that selection process.

### I09 — Cohen and Frazzini (2008), Economic Links and Predictable Returns

Lauren Cohen; Andrea Frazzini. *Journal of Finance* 63(4), 1977–2011. [Author institution](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=31700); [Coauthor research page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Economic-Links-and-Predictable-Returns).

**Access:** author/institutional research records inspected. **Supports:** historical customer–supplier return predictability is consistent with limited attention to public economic relationships. **Limit:** published historical results do not establish current after-cost returns. Links must be dated to their public availability, and tests must control for own momentum, industries, liquidity, and shared exposures before crediting semantic propagation.

### I10 — DellaVigna and Pollet (2009), Investor Inattention and Friday Earnings Announcements

Stefano DellaVigna; Joshua M. Pollet. *Journal of Finance* 64(2), 709–749. [Author institution](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/investor-inattention-and-friday-earnings-announcements/); [Author manuscript](https://sdellavi.com/pdf/earnfr080204.pdf).

**Access:** primary institutional research record inspected. **Supports:** the sample's Friday announcements have weaker immediate response, stronger delayed response, and lower volume, supporting an attention interpretation. **Limit:** announcement timing is selected and the effect may decay. Test attention proxies and alternative explanations; a calendar label alone neither identifies the mechanism nor establishes a presently executable trade.

### I11 — Tetlock (2011), All the News That's Fit to Reprint: Do Investors React to Stale Information?

Paul C. Tetlock. *Review of Financial Studies* 24(5), 1481–1512. [Author institution](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/all-news-thats-fit-reprint-do-investors-react-stale-information); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhq141).

**Access:** primary institutional research record inspected. **Supports:** textual similarity identifies stale stories whose associated price moves predict reversals, particularly with greater individual-investor activity. **Limit:** similarity is an imperfect novelty measure, and repeated information can acquire new relevance. Preserve provenance, first-publication times, and revisions; syndicated copies must not become independent Bayesian likelihood updates.

### I12 — Budish, Cramton, and Shim (2015), The High-Frequency Trading Arms Race: Frequent Batch Auctions as a Market Design Response

Eric Budish; Peter Cramton; John Shim. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 130(4), 1547–1621. [Author research page](https://ericbudish.org/publication/the-high-frequency-trading-arms-race-frequent-batch-auctions-as-a-market-design-response/).

**Access:** author research page and primary results inspected. **Supports:** continuous serial processing can create speed rents even with symmetrically public information; batch auctions change the competitive margin. **Limit:** market-design findings do not establish that a generic bot can capture those rents. Compare opportunity lifetime with measured observation, decision, submission, acknowledgement, and fill delays.

## Risk, constraints, and implementation

### R1 — Cochrane, Asset Pricing, author-hosted sample chapters (copyright 2001)

John H. Cochrane. [Author page](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/asset-pricing); [Sample chapters](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/samplechapters-dy5d.pdf). A revised edition appeared in 2005; this retrieved file was **not verified as that edition**.

**Access:** sample-chapter text, copyright page, and pricing equations inspected. **Supports:** expected excess return relates to covariance with the stochastic discount factor, not raw variance alone. **Limit:** the identity becomes an empirical risk explanation only with a specified pricing kernel and market assumptions. Name the economically bad state and whether the strategy loses when capital is most valuable.

### R2 — Shleifer and Vishny (1997), The Limits of Arbitrage

Andrei Shleifer; Robert W. Vishny. *Journal of Finance* 52(1), 35–55. [Published PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~piazzesi/Reading/ShleiferVishny1997.pdf); [Author record](https://shleifer.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/limits-arbitrage).

**Access:** published opening sections inspected. **Supports:** specialized arbitrage capital can shrink after losses as investors withdraw precisely when price discrepancies widen. **Limit:** persistent spreads can also reflect omitted risk or contract differences. Specify interim mark-to-market loss, collateral, investor withdrawals, and convergence uncertainty; a correct terminal view does not prevent forced liquidation before convergence.

### R3 — Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009), Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity

Markus K. Brunnermeier; Lasse Heje Pedersen. *Review of Financial Studies* 22, 2201–2238. [Author page](https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/publications/market-liquidity-and-funding-liquidity); [Paper](https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/document/98).

**Access:** abstract, model, and margin sections inspected. **Supports:** capital losses, funding constraints, and market illiquidity can reinforce one another, creating shared liquidity exposures. **Limit:** liquidity spirals are conditional outcomes rather than inevitable responses to volatility. Stress joint price loss, spreads, haircuts, margin, and liquidation; initial broker margin is not an adequate capital requirement for strategy design.

### R4 — He, Kelly, and Manela (2017), Intermediary Asset Pricing: New Evidence from Many Asset Classes

Zhiguo He; Bryan Kelly; Asaf Manela. *Journal of Financial Economics* 126, 1–35. [Published PDF](https://mfm.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/He-et-al_Intermediary-asset-pricing-New-evidence-from-many-asset-classes.pdf).

**Access:** published abstract and introductory empirical framework inspected. **Supports:** innovations in primary-dealer capital ratios explain cross-sectional expected returns across the studied asset classes. **Limit:** explanatory evidence is not a reliable live timing rule. Account for release lags, composition changes, and uncertainty, and test whether apparently diversified strategies all depend on healthy intermediary balance sheets.

### R5 — Gârleanu, Pedersen, and Poteshman (2009), Demand-Based Option Pricing

Nicolae Gârleanu; Lasse Heje Pedersen; Allen M. Poteshman. *Review of Financial Studies* 22(10), 4259–4299. [January 2006 manuscript](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/26791/2/S-DRP-06-01.pdf); [Publication record](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/DemandBased-Option-Pricing).

**Access:** 2006 manuscript theorem and residual-hedging-risk sections; 2009 publication verified. **Supports:** demand affects prices through unhedgeable variance and covariance of residual hedging risk. **Limit:** volume and open interest do not reveal net customer demand or dealer inventory. Separate compensation for bearing residual option risk from unusually favorable pricing relative to that same risk.

### R6 — Coval and Stafford (2007), Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets

Joshua Coval; Erik Stafford. *Journal of Financial Economics* 86, 479–512. [Published PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Asset%20Fire%20Sales%20in%20Equity%20Markets_24af6e8c-8417-48a3-a384-053b90473e78.pdf).

**Access:** published abstract and discussion inspected. **Supports:** flow-driven mutual-fund transactions create price pressure in overlapping holdings in the 1980–2004 sample. **Limit:** disclosure lags and endogenous flows complicate replication; older manuscript samples differ. Identify observable obligations to trade using decision-time information rather than calling every decline a forced sale or using subsequently disclosed holdings.

### R7 — Frazzini and Pedersen (2014), Betting Against Beta

Andrea Frazzini; Lasse Heje Pedersen. *Journal of Financial Economics* 111, 1–25. [Published PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Betting%20Against%20Beta%20-%20Frazzini%20and%20Pedersen.pdf).

**Access:** introduction and proposition summaries inspected. **Supports:** leverage constraints can elevate high-beta asset prices; the studied low-beta/high-beta construction earns returns but suffers when funding constraints tighten. **Limit:** beta neutrality is neither funding neutrality nor tail neutrality. Financing, short access, estimation error, and mandate-specific constraints determine whether the proposed investor can capture the documented mechanism.

### R8 — Bollerslev, Tauchen, and Zhou (2009), Expected Stock Returns and Variance Risk Premia

Tim Bollerslev; George Tauchen; Hao Zhou. *Review of Financial Studies* 22(11), 4463–4492. [Publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/11/4463/1565787); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhp008).

**Access:** publisher abstract and bibliographic information; full paper not read in this pass. **Supports:** implied-versus-realized variation relates to subsequent aggregate returns, especially intermediate quarterly horizons in the study. **Limit:** this is not evidence for 0DTE selling. Distinguish risk-neutral variance expectations, physical forecasts, and later realized variance; their differences contain different economic and statistical components.

### R9 — Duffie (2010), Presidential Address: Asset Price Dynamics with Slow-Moving Capital

Darrell Duffie. *Journal of Finance* 65(4), 1237–1267. [Author-hosted published PDF](https://www.darrellduffie.com/uploads/1/4/8/0/148007615/duffieafapresidentialaddress2010.pdf).

**Access:** abstract and opening framework inspected. **Supports:** search and capital-raising frictions can create sharp price responses followed by extended reversals as capital reaches opportunities slowly. **Limit:** this does not justify fading every information shock. Specify the source of the imbalance, mechanism of capital arrival, convergence-time uncertainty, and the funding required to survive the intervening path.

### R10 — Frazzini, Israel, and Moskowitz (August 2018 working paper), Trading Costs

Andrea Frazzini; Ronen Israel; Tobias J. Moskowitz. [Institution-hosted manuscript](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/Trading-Cost.pdf); [Author submission](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3229719).

**Access:** abstract and execution/measurement sections inspected. **Supports:** institutional executions permit estimates of impact and implementation shortfall, including missed-trade costs. **Limit:** the studied executions were almost always completed, and stock-lending fees are excluded. These costs are not universal for small stocks, retail options, or fast signals. Estimate size, urgency, adverse selection, nonfills, and borrow separately.

### R11 — Gabaix and Koijen (May 12, 2022 manuscript), In Search of the Origins of Financial Fluctuations: The Inelastic Markets Hypothesis

Xavier Gabaix; Ralph S. J. Koijen. [Retrieved manuscript](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/SSRN-id3686935.pdf); [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28967).

**Access:** manuscript abstract, identification, and robustness passages inspected. **Supports:** relatively inelastic institutional demand can amplify market-level flow effects, including through portfolio mandates. **Limit:** estimated aggregate multipliers are not universal intraday or stock-specific impact constants. Distinguish temporary liquidity shocks from durable allocations, and treat inferred demand elasticities and participant identities as uncertain estimates.

## Betting, forecasting, and allocation

### B01 — Kelly (1956), A New Interpretation of Information Rate

J. L. Kelly Jr. *Bell System Technical Journal* 35(4), 917–926. [Publisher](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/a-new-interpretation-of-information-rate/); [Article reproduced with permission](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2017/07/Kelly1956-1uwz47o.pdf).

**Access:** article text inspected. **Supports:** connects information about gamble outcomes with attainable long-run log wealth growth at offered odds. **Limit:** optimal allocation depends on the payoff and probability model; the ideal result does not remove costs, estimation error, limited horizons, or constraints. Correct directional forecasts can still lose wealth through miscalibration or excessive stakes.

### B02 — Thorp (1997; revised May 29, 1998), The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting, and the Stock Market

Edward O. Thorp. Conference paper. [Primary text and chapter links](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/M330/thorp/paper.htm).

**Access:** abstract and structure read; chapter PDFs retrieved but yielded no extracted text. **Supports:** separates finding favorable bets from deciding their size and discusses growth-optimal allocation across gambling and securities. **Limit:** Thorp's historical success does not validate a new system. Kelly, martingale staking, or equity smoothing cannot by itself turn a negative-expectation payoff into positive-expectation alpha.

### B03 — Gneiting and Raftery (2007), Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation

Tilmann Gneiting; Adrian E. Raftery. *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 102(477), 359–378. [Author PDF](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf).

**Access:** original paper text inspected. **Supports:** strictly proper scores reward honest probabilistic forecasts in expectation and connect forecasting with entropy, calibration, and sharpness. **Limit:** scoring improvement does not imply executable profit, especially on the selected trade subset. Maintain separate forecast and net-policy evaluations; financial chronology and dependence require suitable validation rather than indiscriminate random folds.

### B04 — Wolfers and Zitzewitz (2006), Interpreting Prediction Market Prices as Probabilities

Justin Wolfers; Eric Zitzewitz. FRBSF Working Paper 2006-11 / NBER 12200. [Primary paper](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp06-11bk.pdf).

**Access:** paper sections on utility, wealth, and equilibrium inspected. **Supports:** prices can equal mean beliefs under specific conditions; correlated wealth and beliefs produce wealth-weighted aggregation. **Limit:** average belief is not objective truth, and hedging, utility, restrictions, and costs affect demand. Treat price as a strong benchmark with explicit assumptions, never automatic proof for or against model disagreement.

### B05 — Snowberg and Wolfers (2010), Explaining the Favorite–Long Shot Bias: Is It Risk-Love or Misperceptions?

Erik Snowberg; Justin Wolfers. *Journal of Political Economy* 118(4), 723–746. [Primary working-paper repository](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/4884/explaining-the-favorite-longshot-bias-is-it-risk-love-or-misperceptions).

**Access:** primary abstract read; NBER PDF indexed but direct retrieval denied. **Supports:** compound-bet choices help distinguish risk preference from probability misperception, favoring the latter in the studied horse-racing data. **Limit:** this does not establish universal longshot overpricing or profitable favorites after house costs. Test mechanism-specific predictions and actual net value in the target market.

### B06 — Levitt (2004), Why Are Gambling Markets Organised So Differently from Financial Markets?

Steven D. Levitt. *Economic Journal* 114(495), 223–246. [Author/university PDF](https://pricetheory.uchicago.edu/levitt/Papers/LevittWhyAreGamblingMarkets2004.pdf).

**Access:** original article text inspected. **Supports:** bookmakers in the studied setting deliberately take outcome exposure and exploit bettor biases instead of only balancing wagers. **Limit:** dated sportsbook evidence is not a universal description of current exchanges. A counterparty accepting inventory may also be better informed; observed imbalances alone do not establish uninformed flow or a hedging need.

### B07 — Busseti, Ryu, and Boyd (2016), Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling

Enzo Busseti; Ernest K. Ryu; Stephen Boyd. *Journal of Investing* 25(3), 118–134. [Author PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf).

**Access:** model and drawdown framework inspected. **Supports:** a tractable conservative bound can constrain drawdown probability while optimizing log growth. **Limit:** the core repeated-return model assumes IID draws; the initial-wealth floor is not a rolling peak-to-trough maximum-drawdown guarantee. Unmodeled dependence, tails, collateral, or changing regimes invalidate any unsupported claim of real-market protection.

### B08 — Sun and Boyd (2018 preprint; revised 2021), Distributional Robust Kelly Gambling: Optimal Strategy under Uncertainty in the Long-Run

Qingyun Sun; Stephen Boyd. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.10371), version 3 dated June 10, 2021. Also cited as **V9**.

**Access:** author preprint abstract and version information inspected. **Supports:** optimize worst-case expected log growth over a specified set of distributions; useful finite-state cases are tractable. **Limit:** protection extends only to the specified ambiguity set and wealth model. Too narrow a set creates false reassurance; broad sets can favor cash. An unexplained fractional-Kelly haircut is not this result.

### B09 — Hanson (2007; author draft January 2002), Logarithmic Market Scoring Rules for Modular Combinatorial Information Aggregation

Robin Hanson. *Journal of Prediction Markets* 1(1), 3–15. [Author draft](https://hanson.gmu.edu/mktscore.pdf); [Published paper](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/view/417/448).

**Access:** author draft inspected; publication version verified. **Supports:** market scoring rules combine forecasting incentives with group information aggregation and useful conditional consistency properties. **Limit:** information subsidies are paid by someone, and marginal quotes change with size. Classify rebates and sponsored liquidity separately from persistent alpha, and integrate the execution price curve rather than extrapolating the first quote.

### B10 — Cover (1991), Universal Portfolios

Thomas M. Cover. *Mathematical Finance* 1(1), 1–29. [Original article](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper93.pdf); [Inspected author version dated October 23, 1996](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/universal_portfolios.pdf).

**Access:** author version inspected; 1991 publication verified. **Supports:** a nonanticipating allocation asymptotically matches the exponential growth rate of the best constant-rebalanced portfolio in hindsight under the specified model. **Limit:** relative regret is not guaranteed positive absolute return, finite-horizon superiority, or costless implementation. Distinguish diversification and allocation improvements from new predictive information and evaluate feasible benchmarks.

### B11 — Brown and Sandholm (2019), Superhuman AI for Multiplayer Poker

Noam Brown; Tuomas Sandholm. *Science* 365(6456), 885–890. [Author paper](https://noambrown.github.io/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf).

**Access:** original paper's game-theory discussion inspected. **Supports:** strong multiplayer poker performance and the distinction between equilibrium robustness and opponent exploitation; special two-player zero-sum guarantees do not transfer automatically. **Limit:** financial markets have changing participants, uncertain payoff models, and external utility. Poker success is not evidence of LLM market alpha; model counter-adaptation and the disappearance of exploitable behavior.

### B12 — Kalshi (March 17, 2026), Market Rules; Market Outcomes

Official documentation group: [Market Rules](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823822-market-rules); [Market Outcomes](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823826-market-outcomes). Two primary pages, counted as one documentation entry.

**Access:** both pages read. **Supports:** individual contracts specify resolution criteria and information sources; determination need not coincide with trading closure. **Limit:** general help pages are not a particular contract's terms. Verify cutoff, inclusivity, revisions, exceptions, settlement, collateral, and current fees for the exact instrument. Similar headlines do not establish economically equivalent payoffs or arbitrage.

## Validation, behavior, and adaptation

### V1 — White (2000), A Reality Check for Data Snooping

Halbert White. *Econometrica* 68(5), 1097–1126. [Original paper](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~bhansen/718/White2000.pdf).

**Access:** original paper inspected. **Supports:** judging a selected winner requires accounting for the candidate search, rather than testing it as the only attempted model. **Limit:** inference only reflects the recorded candidate universe and the procedure's dependence assumptions. Hidden failures and later revisions remain material; predictive superiority neither identifies a causal mechanism nor proves economically sufficient executable returns.

### V2 — Hansen (2005), A Test for Superior Predictive Ability

Peter Reinhard Hansen. *Journal of Business & Economic Statistics* 23(4), 365–380. [Author research page](https://reinhardhansen.github.io/research.html); [Primary submission](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=264569).

**Access:** author/SSRN abstracts inspected; publisher open unavailable. **Supports:** SPA uses studentization and a sample-dependent null to reduce sensitivity to irrelevant poor alternatives. **Limit:** rejecting the global null establishes superiority somewhere in the candidate family, not automatic certification of its selected winner. Preserve joint dependence during resampling and prespecify the principal test instead of selecting favorable diagnostics.

### V3 — Bailey and López de Prado (2014), The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality

David H. Bailey; Marcos López de Prado. Author version July 31, 2014. [Paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

**Access:** author paper inspected. **Supports:** assesses Sharpe inflation using selection, track-record length, non-normality, and the distribution of trials. **Limit:** it is a model-based significance diagnostic, not a posterior probability of live profit. Unknown search histories, leakage, dependency misspecification, stale executions, and regime change remain unresolved. Document the effective trial count rather than treating correlated variants as independent.

### V4 — Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, and Zhu (February 27, 2015 author version), The Probability of Backtest Overfitting

David H. Bailey; Jonathan M. Borwein; Marcos López de Prado; Qiji Jim Zhu. [Author paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf).

**Access:** paper and rank-based definition inspected. **Supports:** combinatorially symmetric cross-validation estimates selection instability: how often the in-sample winner ranks below the out-of-sample median. **Limit:** PBO is not the probability of losing money; one minus PBO is not a profit probability. Combinatorial partitions do not replace chronological evaluation reproducing every live refit, selection decision, and execution restriction.

### V5 — Lo (2004), The Adaptive Markets Hypothesis: Market Efficiency from an Evolutionary Perspective

Andrew W. Lo. *Journal of Portfolio Management*, 30th anniversary issue. [Author paper](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/JPM2004_Pub.pdf).

**Access:** primary paper inspected. **Supports:** competition, capital, institutions, and conditions can change efficiency and strategy opportunity sets. **Limit:** this developing framework is not a quantitative law supplying an optimal regime variable or adaptation speed. Test those choices prospectively; automatic retuning after losses may merely repackage data mining as adaptation, and changed conditions need not explain every drawdown.

### V6 — Odean (1998), Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?

Terrance Odean. *Journal of Finance* 53(5), 1775–1798. [Author paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf).

**Access:** primary paper inspected. **Supports:** records for 10,000 accounts show a disposition toward realizing gains over losses that common alternative explanations do not adequately explain in the sample. **Limit:** individual bias does not establish a tradable price effect. Show that affected holders influence the marginal price, can be identified using available data, and create a persistent net opportunity.

### V7 — Barber and Odean (2000), Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth: The Common Stock Investment Performance of Individual Investors

Brad M. Barber; Terrance Odean. *Journal of Finance* 55(2), 773–806. [Author paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf).

**Access:** paper and abstract inspected. **Supports:** highly active households in the studied 1991–1996 brokerage sample had worse net performance, consistent with overconfidence and trading costs. **Limit:** the association is not experimental proof of every trade's motivation, and historical retail costs are not current universal costs. Activity and confident language are not evidence of incremental net utility.

### V8 — Dawid (1984), Present Position and Potential Developments: Some Personal Views—Statistical Theory: The Prequential Approach

A. Philip Dawid. *JRSS Series A* 147(2), 278–290. [Publisher](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssa/article/147/2/278/7106293); [Paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jrennie/trg/papers/dawid-prequential-84.pdf).

**Access:** publisher summary inspected. **Supports:** assess inference through sequential forecasts issued before future observations; this motivates timestamped prospective prediction records. **Limit:** calibration alone is insufficient: a base-rate model can be calibrated but uninformative, and an informative model may be untradeable at prevailing prices. Compare market-relative forecast quality separately from execution and decision value.

### V9 — Alias of B08: Sun and Boyd, Distributional Robust Kelly Gambling

Use **B08** for the exact source, 2018/2021 version information, access description, supported result, and limitations. This stable cross-reference is preserved for validation materials but **does not count as a second source**. Distributional ambiguity must be represented explicitly; it is not interchangeable with a posterior probability interval, a generic haircut, or a guarantee against unmodeled losses.

### V10 — Dawid (2020), Decision-theoretic Foundations for Statistical Causality

A. Philip Dawid. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12493).

**Access:** author paper record inspected. **Supports:** transferring observational evidence to intervention decisions requires justified relationships between observational and decision regimes. **Limit:** profitable prediction need not identify every causal parameter, but attributing the opportunity to a specific participant or mechanism requires stronger evidence than fit. Trading at scale can itself alter the relevant regime through market impact and strategic response.

### V11 — McLean and Pontiff (2016), Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?

R. David McLean; Jeffrey Pontiff. *Journal of Finance* 71(1), 5–32; first online 2015. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12365).

**Access:** publisher abstract inspected. **Supports:** returns to 97 predictors declined out of sample and further after publication, consistent partly with publication-informed trading; predictor correlations also increased. **Limit:** sample averages are not a universal haircut or proof every public strategy fails. Diagnose current durability and crowding prospectively rather than converting historical decay percentages into fixed rules.

### V12 — Chen, Lopez-Lira, and Zimmermann (2022 working paper), Does Peer-Reviewed Research Help Predict Stock Returns?

Andrew Y. Chen; Alejandro Lopez-Lira; Tom Zimmermann. [Retrieved working-paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317). No claim that this is the latest journal version.

**Access:** author working-paper record inspected. **Supports:** the studied data-mined accounting predictors can have post-sample performance comparable to published predictors, challenging theory labels as automatic durability evidence. **Limit:** this is a defined comparison, not a theorem that mechanisms are useless. Use mechanisms for falsification and transfer diagnostics, while requiring measurement rather than substituting persuasive economic stories for evidence.

## Additional foundations

### S1 — Sharpe (1991), The Arithmetic of Active Management

William F. Sharpe. *Financial Analysts Journal* 47(1), 7–9. [Author text](https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/active/active.htm).

**Access:** primary author text inspected. **Supports:** for the defined market and accounting conventions, aggregate active management matches passive holdings before costs and underperforms after higher costs. **Limit:** aggregate arithmetic neither excludes individual skill nor identifies exploitable counterparties. State the market, benchmark, investor group, and costs; a positive active return can still be a negative contribution relative to opportunity cost.

### S2 — Pedersen (2018), Sharpening the Arithmetic of Active Management

Lasse Heje Pedersen. *Financial Analysts Journal* 74(1), 21–36. [Publisher/research page](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2018/faj-v74-n1-4).

**Access:** primary publication abstract inspected. **Supports:** changing securities supply and real implementation complicate static active-versus-passive arithmetic; passive portfolios also need trading. **Limit:** qualifying an accounting benchmark does not prove a selected active strategy earns net alpha. Define feasible replication, issuance/reconstitution trading, and costs before using a frictionless passive benchmark or claiming active management necessarily adds value.

### S3 — Gârleanu and Pedersen (2013), Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs

Nicolae Gârleanu; Lasse Heje Pedersen. *Journal of Finance* 68(6), 2309–2340; NBER working paper 15205 (2009). [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205); [March 2012 author manuscript](https://nbgarleanu.github.io/DynTrad.pdf).

**Access:** primary abstract verified and March 2012 author PDF retrieved; NBER direct open returned 403. **Supports:** optimal trading balances predictive-return decay with trading costs and future target positions. **Limit:** a model-derived policy depends on its signal, cost, and risk assumptions. Faster turnover need not improve capture when additional impact overwhelms the signal retained.

### S4 — Frazzini, Israel, and Moskowitz (2012 working paper), Trading Costs of Asset Pricing Anomalies

Andrea Frazzini; Ronen Israel; Tobias J. Moskowitz. [Author research page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs-of-Asset-Pricing-Anomalies).

**Access:** primary research abstract inspected. **Supports:** institutional execution data can change assessments of anomaly costs, implementation, and capacity; portfolio construction matters for attainable returns. **Limit:** institutional execution evidence is not a universal fee or impact schedule for another desk. Related authorship and research lineage with R10 do not constitute independent confirmation. Re-estimate costs for the intended assets, scale, and urgency.
