What could earn the return?

HYPOTHESIS: The desk sells downside insurance to participants willing to pay to reduce crash exposure. Their risk reduction can be rational; we need not assume they make forecasting mistakes. Our potential contribution is patient, adequately funded capital willing to absorb losses when protection becomes valuable. Whether this desk actually has that advantage is UNKNOWN. An indicator combination is a selection rule, not evidence of superior crash forecasting.

The proposed wedge is option premium minus the physical expected spread payout, trading costs, financing, and the opportunity cost of tied-up capital. Its strongest rivals are ordinary short-volatility/downside exposure, a lucky absence or underweighting of rare losses, and optimization over noise. The adverse state is a falling, illiquid market with correlated spread losses and limited exit liquidity, possibly coinciding with losses elsewhere in the desk. Capacity is limited by executable spread markets and stressed collateral, not just average trading volume. This is risky insurance provision, not arbitrage.

DERIVATION: For an ideal same-expiry put credit spread with short strike K_H, long strike K_L < K_H, width W = K_H − K_L, credit C per share, contract multiplier M, and terminal underlying S_T,

    L(S_T) = min(W, max(K_H − S_T, 0))
    terminal net P&L = M[C − L(S_T)] − total costs.

Thus positive expected cash P&L requires C > E[L(S_T)] + costs/M. Discounting and capital opportunity cost must also be included for the funded-return comparison. The ideal terminal maximum loss is M(W − C) before costs; early exercise, assignment, incomplete legs, settlement, and interim funding require their own path analysis. This formula does not assert the unspecified contracts have cash settlement or eliminate operational exposure.

With average gross winning amount G and average losing amount L, the supplied 98% win rate would imply gross expectancy 0.98G − 0.02L. It is negative when L > 49G; costs lower that threshold. Neither that rate nor the Sharpe supplies the missing loss distribution. Do not annualize by treating overlapping or correlated positions as independent.

B — New research strategy: customer guidance to supplier repricing

This is a specific, untested research hypothesis suited to your data and latency constraints: buy a supplier after a customer makes a new, demand-driven upward revenue-guidance revision, but only if a modest model predicts remaining supplier value after the customer's and supplier's observable price responses and all trading costs. Use a five-session holding horizon and long-only positions. The evidence stage is HYPOTHESIS; no attached market dataset means forecast quality, net returns and capacity remain UNKNOWN.

Candidate selection

• Information/inference: interpret new customer demand information through previously public supplier exposure. Available releases and as-of links make this the best first experiment. Risk: the price has already adjusted or the link does not transmit demand.
• Risk/service provision: provide liquidity to suppliers during earnings-related order imbalances. Plausible in principle, but the available inputs do not identify compelled flow or queue outcomes. Defer until the desk has a defensible flow measure and execution evidence.
• Access/constraints: specialize in economically meaningful, small-capacity supplier links too small to support a large desk's research expense. This could enable the first mechanism; it is not a separate profit term. Public-link processing costs, small feasible size and limited attention are hypotheses, not proof that large competitors ignore the names.

The selected edge contract

Participant: investors and liquidity providers pricing a supplier whose customers disclose new demand evidence. Proposed belief wedge: they incompletely translate that evidence into supplier revenues at the time we can act. Our proposed advantage is accurate relationship mapping and cheap, auditable extraction, not winning the first milliseconds. Persistence would require useful information to remain after a 30-minute processing delay, at enough attainable size to cover costs. Bad states include a common sector shock, customer inventory accumulation, supplier substitution, concentration, and liquidity drying up. The strongest rival is ordinary customer momentum or common-sector news rather than economic-link interpretation.

Observable implications: stronger publicly disclosed customer exposure should predict a stronger supplier response; the model should add value beyond the customer's return and the supplier's own move; delayed entry should not leave all apparent value before our feasible decision; repeated stale releases should add no independent evidence. Failure of these tests weakens the proposed mechanism even if an unexplained pattern remains.

Frozen v0 research specification

The following numerical choices are MODEL ASSUMPTIONS for a first experiment, not optimized values or approved live limits. Register them before viewing outcomes; changes create new trials.

1. Universe: ordinary listed shares with a verified, previously public customer→supplier relationship. Exclude changes explained only by acquisition, currency, price increases, accounting changes or EPS/margin effects. Positive customer profits alone need not benefit suppliers. Missing comparability, ambiguous transmission or ambiguous entity resolution means no trade. Source text, prior guidance, extraction version and supporting spans are stored.
