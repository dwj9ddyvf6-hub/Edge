# The consensus lens: examine every object against the consensus it has to beat

This reference is the audit instrument for a research estate that has accumulated more claims than it can grade.
It operationalizes the consensus-circumvention critique (Morillo's framework, a published third-party source) into
eight tests, a twelve-field card and four verdicts, and it was run over one desk's full estate on 14 and 15
November 2026: 199 research directories and 119 report files, a 339-sleeve roster, a 641-row peer catalog, the
methods inventory and the data assets, each object carded or named as an open row.

Read this file when any object is about to be called proven, when a research corpus needs grading rather than
summarizing, when a new claim arrives from any source (a model, a peer, the literature, your own last wave), and
when a portfolio of strategies must be separated into what merely restates the consensus and what might actually
differ from it. A claim that has not faced the lens is not false; it is unexamined, and unexamined claims compound
silently into sizing, promotion and kill decisions downstream.

## The governing claim

**A strategy does not beat the consensus by agreeing with it more confidently.** Every object in an estate is
consensus-grade until it names the consensus it departs from, the source of the departure, and the evidence that
the departure survives an honest count of everything that was tried to find it. Most objects fail not on their
numbers but on their paperwork: no baseline, no denominator, a hypothesis selected by the same process that
generated it on the same data that inspired it. The lens makes that paperwork the grade.

## The eight tests

Apply all eight, on evidence, not on argument. An object that cannot face a test records the gap; it does not
skip the test.

- **T1, Consensus baseline named.** What IS the consensus for this object, and how is it measured? Admissible
  baselines: analyst consensus, the options-implied move, the fleet median forecast, the published literature
  claim, the model's own modal answer, SPY/QQQ buy-and-hold. No baseline, no grade: an object that cannot name
  its baseline is UNGRADABLE-YET by definition.
- **T2, Difference source identified.** Where would a non-consensus view come from? Admissible sources: private
  data (T3), preserved disagreement (T7), a decomposition nobody prices, timing or latency, structure (the choice
  of expression), or a selection process over generated volume (T4). "The model thinks so" is not a difference
  source.
- **T3, Private-data conditioning.** Does the object consume data that exists in no training set: your fills,
  near-miss logs, signal journals, disagreement records, the book's own ledger? Score NONE, INCIDENTAL or CORE.
  An object conditioned on data only this estate has is the strongest difference claim there is, because the
  consensus cannot have priced what it cannot see.
- **T4, Generation and selection separated.** Who generated the hypothesis, and who selects? Selection must be
  sealed, forward and mechanical: a pre-registered falsifier, a fixed checkpoint. A hypothesis selected by the
  process that generated it, on the data that inspired it, fails T4 regardless of its backtest.
- **T5, Trial accounting.** The denominator is the asset. Every object carries nominal trials, hidden trials
  (variants tried and discarded, parameter neighbors, the research waves that produced it), a correlation
  discount to effective N, and a deflated Sharpe at effective N wherever a Sharpe is claimed. Compute the
  best-of-N inflation, never wave at it: the best of 1,000 null trials shows a Sharpe near 3.26 by luck alone.
- **T6, Intermediate-outcome scoring.** Verdicts at sleeve level take longer than careers (roughly 785 resolved
  bets to detect a 55 percent hit rate; roughly 31 years to verdict one Sharpe 0.5 sleeve alone). So verdicts
  live at fleet and cohort level and on fast-resolving intermediate outcomes: earnings surprises, next-day moves,
  event outcomes, signal-day follow-through. Every object names its intermediate outcome and its scoring rule
  (IC, Brier, hit/against/silent) at intake.
- **T7, Disagreement preserved, not debated away.** Debate converges to voting and can converge sycophantically.
  Disagreement between models, between research nodes and across a fleet is logged as a data object with its own
  series, bucketed, and tested for monotonicity against outcomes. It is never averaged away before scoring.
- **T8, Decay and crowding monitored.** Post-publication decay (McLean and Pontiff: roughly 58 percent) and
  crowding apply to your finds too. Every surviving object carries a crowding gauge (is this now the consensus
  trade?) and a decay check at each re-grade.

## The Lens Card: one per object

Twelve fields; no card is complete with a field blank. A blank is recorded as UNGRADABLE-YET with the missing
field named.

1. Object and provenance (who built it, when, from what).
2. Consensus baseline (T1) and how it is measured.
3. Claimed difference (T2), in one sentence.
4. Private data consumed (T3): NONE, INCIDENTAL or CORE, with the asset named.
5. Generation source (model, human, literature, mutation of what parent).
6. Selection history (T4): sealed or open, forward or in-sample.
7. Trial denominator (T5): nominal, hidden estimate, effective N, DSR where claimed.
8. Forward falsifier, sample size and checkpoint date. An object without one does not enter or stay in the
   forward population.
9. Intermediate outcome and scoring rule (T6).
10. Disagreement record (T7): where this object sits in current dispersion.
11. Crowding and decay status (T8).
12. Framework verdict, grader and date.

## The four verdicts

- **CONSENSUS-ONLY:** no difference source survives inspection. The object may still run, but it is labeled as a
  consensus expression and sized as one.
- **DIFFERENCE-PLAUSIBLE:** a real difference source exists; forward proof pending.
- **DIFFERENCE-PROVEN-FORWARD:** the pre-registered forward falsifier checkpoint passed on resolved samples.
- **UNGRADABLE-YET:** baseline or denominator missing; the missing piece is named, with an owner and a date.

## Running an examination

1. **Freeze and manifest.** Build the corpus manifest and reconcile it: research claims extracted as separate
   objects (a report is not graded as a unit; its claims are), rosters frozen at counted numbers, methods and
   data assets listed. Any count that cannot be reconciled is named, not rounded. Create the trial register: one
   row per object, denominator fields empty until the audit fills them. Rows are appended, never deleted; a
   re-grade adds a dated note to the row.
2. **Baselines.** For each family, define the T1 baseline and compute it where the data exists: fleet median
   forecasts from the signal journals, implied moves for earnings objects, published claims for literature
   imports, SPY/QQQ for everything.
3. **Cards.** Card the decision-relevant population first: everything firing, everything feeding a book, every
   queued candidate, every method. The long tail gets family-level cards with per-object rows in the register.
   Gate: card count equals manifest count for the scoped population, or the gap is named per object.
4. **The multiple-comparisons audit.** For every object carrying a performance claim, reconstruct the hidden
   trial count and re-grade the headline at DSR with its true effective N. Nothing is killed in this phase: a
   claim that falls is relabeled to its honest grade, and its forward falsifier, if any, still runs.

## Checks and failure modes

- An examination that grades reports instead of claims has graded nothing.
- A verdict upgraded because the forward window is inconvenient is a falsifier rewritten after the result: a
  rationalization, not a grade.
- External literature designs the test; it is never the verdict. Kills come only from your own resolved samples
  hitting pre-registered falsifiers.
- The examination is read-only on strategies: it pauses, resizes and rewrites nothing. Changes flow only through
  the falsifier and promotion machinery the estate already runs.

## Evidence

The 2026-11-14/15 application seeded the register with its priority rows and printed their state honestly: the
estate's flagship find (an overnight basket premium, DSR 0.99994 as found) entered as DIFFERENCE-PLAUSIBLE with
its re-grade first in the Phase 3 queue, the queue being to count every decomposition, leg, basket variant and
friction level the hunt that produced it had examined. A sealed held-out slice of 9,491 rows carried a
DIFFERENCE-PLAUSIBLE card for a ledger-versus-surface object, its grade dated rather than assumed. Candidates
whose owners had not confirmed them sat at UNGRADABLE-YET with the missing confirmation named, not silently
promoted. The lens's first product was not a verdict; it was an estate that could finally say, per object, what
it knew, what it merely believed, and what it had never counted.
