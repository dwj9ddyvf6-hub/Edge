# Literature map: field lessons beside published work

Each field lesson from the [casebook](field-casebook.md) beside published work it confirms, qualifies or contradicts.
Every entry below was checked on 26 October 2026 against Crossref metadata (authors, title, year, journal, volume and
issue) or, for Leitch and Tanner, the RePEc record. Earlier registers stay in [sources.md](sources.md) (I, R, B, V,
S) and [frontier-sources.md](frontier-sources.md). A published result holds under its own sample and assumptions; it
does not make a present strategy profitable.

## Field lessons

| Case | Published work | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| [C01](field-casebook.md#c01-volatility-is-the-most-predictable-thing-on-a-chart) volatility is predictable | Engle (1982); Corsi (2009) | Confirms: volatility clusters and is forecastable; Corsi's HAR model is the own-history baseline the desk used |
| [C02](field-casebook.md#c02-small-cap-buy-triggers-are-traps-negative-selection-pays) lottery-like stocks lag | Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011); Ang, Hodrick, Xing and Zhang (2006); Boyer, Mitton and Vorkink (2010) | Confirms: extreme recent daily gains, high idiosyncratic volatility and high expected skewness predict lower returns |
| C02, [C09](field-casebook.md#c09-luck-is-where-the-alpha-is-the-predictable-tail-did-not-pay-the-stock-buyer) the payer buys lottery tickets | Barberis and Huang (2008); Barber and Odean (2008) | Confirms the mechanism: probability weighting overprices positively skewed securities; individual investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks |
| [C03](field-casebook.md#c03-chartslector-warnings-work-in-thin-markets-buy-rules-do-not) edges live in thin tiers | Hou, Xue and Zhang (2020) | Confirms the location: many published anomalies weaken once microcaps are down-weighted |
| [C04](field-casebook.md#c04-nested-information-adds-nothing) nested information | Green, Hand and Zhang (2017) | Confirms: of many published characteristics, few carry independent information once the others are controlled for |
| [C05](field-casebook.md#c05-widely-watched-public-data-is-in-the-price) public data is priced | Grossman and Stiglitz (1980), [I01](sources.md) | Consistent: prices reflect costly information only to the extent its rents repay acquiring it; free, widely read data earns little |
| [C06](field-casebook.md#c06-forecast-accuracy-is-not-economic-value) accuracy is not value | Leitch and Tanner (1991) | Confirms: conventional error statistics relate only weakly to the profits a forecast earns |
| [C08](field-casebook.md#c08-fear-priced-above-realized-volatility-paid-the-warehouse) the variance premium | Bollerslev, Tauchen and Zhou (2009); Carr and Wu (2009); Coval and Shumway (2001) | Confirms: implied above realized variance predicts market returns; variance buyers pay a premium; option buyers pay for volatility protection |
| [C10](field-casebook.md#c10-exits-that-cut-the-tail) exits that cut the tail | Kaminski and Lo (2014) | Qualifies: stop-loss rules lower expected returns when prices follow a random walk and can help under momentum or regime shifts; on the desk's daily and weekly holds they hurt |
| [C11](field-casebook.md#c11-kelly-sizing-on-an-edge-found-in-the-same-history) estimated-edge sizing | DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) | Confirms: estimation error lets equal weights match or beat optimized portfolios out of sample |
| [C12](field-casebook.md#c12-edges-are-era-bound) era-bound edges | McLean and Pontiff (2016); Chordia, Subrahmanyam and Tong (2014) | Confirms: anomaly returns fall out of sample and after publication, and weakened in the recent high-liquidity era |
| [C13](field-casebook.md#c13-point-in-time-the-leak-and-the-volume-flaw) point in time | Shumway (1997); Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson and Ross (1992) | Confirms: missing delisting returns bias results; survivorship alone creates apparent persistence |
| [C14](field-casebook.md#c14-reverse-splits-check-the-composition) reverse splits | Desai and Jain (1997) | Confirms the sign (negative long-run returns after reverse splits); the desk's record narrows it to repeat splitters |
| [C15](field-casebook.md#c15-the-165-million-filter-rule) large searches | Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016); Sullivan, Timmermann and White (1999); Bailey and López de Prado (2014) | Confirms: hundreds of tested factors demand higher hurdles; technical rules' significance fades after correcting for the search; Sharpe ratios must be deflated by the number of trials |
| C02 chart patterns | Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992); Lo, Mamaysky and Wang (2000) | Contradicted in the desk's universe: 2 of 4,014 pattern cells CONFIRMED (desk research record, 2026-10-20); consistent with Sullivan, Timmermann and White (1999) |
| [C16](field-casebook.md#c16-per-fill-or-per-opportunity) per fill or per opportunity | Linnainmaa (2010) | Confirms: limit-order fills are adversely selected, so results measured on fills mislead |
| [C17](field-casebook.md#c17-the-cost-stack), [C20](field-casebook.md#c20-an-edge-that-never-executes-is-zero) execution and missed trades | Perold (1988); Novy-Marx and Velikov (2016) | Confirms: the paper portfolio overstates the real one by execution costs and missed trades; costs remove most high-turnover anomalies |
| [C18](field-casebook.md#c18-speed-late-opens-stale-prices-and-the-route-stage) speed | Almgren and Chriss (2001); Budish, Cramton and Shim (2015), [I12](sources.md) | Framework: execution trades impact against timing risk; speed rents attract an arms race |
| [C19](field-casebook.md#c19-crowding-many-sleeves-one-bet) crowding | Khandani and Lo (2011) | Confirms: crowded quantitative strategies lost together when one unwound |
